## P1 (core) — the optional output sink changed the verdict

`validate_warrant_receipt(..., view=None)` neither froze its inputs nor
built the committed-reason view, and the semantic check-blob binding read
`(view or {}).get("committed", {})`. So the *output sink* governed
verification semantics. On a fully resealed countervector — check blob
removed from receipt and universe, descriptor and `bundle_root` recomputed,
the committed reason left as `matched`:

```
view=None  codes= []
view={}    codes= ['CHECK_BLOB_ABSENT']
same_verdict= False
```

Without a sink an impossible "the check was executed" claim was accepted,
and the whole input-isolation layer was skipped along with it.

**Disposition.** The verdict now runs over an **unconditional internal
view** (`_verdict`), and `view` is a pure output sink that receives a copy
**after** the verdict and is never read during it. Freezing is
unconditional; on a freeze refusal the sink is cleared so no consumer can
mistake a partial freeze for a validated one.

Vectors: `view=None`, `view={}` and a populated sink must agree — asserted
on the fixture and on the exact case that exposed the defect (a `matched`
reason whose check blob is absent); plus uncopyable-input refusal proved
**with no sink at all**, so the model suite now covers the freeze rather
than relying on the projector's TOCTOU vectors.

## P2 (core) — the CAS boundary was unbounded

A non-bytes CAS value or an ordinary resolver exception escaped as
`TypeError`/`RuntimeError`. `cas_resolve` now keeps `KeyError` (callers
handle it), turns any other resolver failure into
`SealViolation("CAS_RESOLVER_FAILED")`, and rejects non-bytes values as
`CAS_NOT_BYTES` — normalising `bytearray` to `bytes`. `BaseException` is not
caught.
